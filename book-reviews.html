Piranesi
Susanna Clarke
Fiction · Fantasy
★★★★★

Clarke builds an impossible, luminous world — a house with infinite halls and tidal statues — and populates it with a narrator of heartbreaking gentleness. A meditation on isolation, identity, and the fragility of the self we believe in.

The Dawn of Everything
David Graeber & David Wengrow
Non-fiction · History
★★★★★

A thrilling act of historiographical sabotage. Graeber and Wengrow dismantle the grand narratives of human progress, revealing a past full of political experimentation, democratic moments, and choices far more varied than we've been taught.

Fever Dream
Samanta Schweblin
Fiction · Horror
★★★★½

Compressed, airless, and utterly terrifying. Schweblin writes dread the way other writers write sunsets — with an eye for the precise detail that transforms ordinary things into omens. A masterclass in sustained unease.

A Field Guide to Getting Lost
Rebecca Solnit
Essays · Memoir
★★★★★

Solnit writes about loss, wandering, and the unknown with a precision that feels impossible for subject matter this amorphous. She makes getting lost feel like a practice, a discipline, a way of remaining alive to the world.

Capital in the Twenty-First Century
Thomas Piketty
Non-fiction · Economics
★★★★

Piketty's grand historical argument — that capital tends to accumulate faster than economies grow, producing ever-greater inequality — is compelling even where it's flawed. A book that changed how economists and politicians talk about wealth.

Citizen: An American Lyric
Claudia Rankine
Poetry · Essay
★★★★★

A work that refuses genre like it refuses easy comfort. Rankine moves between poetry, essay, and visual art to document the ongoing, accumulated weight of racial microaggression in America. Necessary and devastating.
